Architecture used with Fabric

Oracle remains the system of record; Fabric adds a governed analytics plane without introducing a scheduled copy pipeline.

  1. 01 Call processing

    PII-safe call record

    Redacted summary, disposition, churn signals, escalation flags, and handle time.

  2. 02 Oracle Database 19c

    System of record

    The existing call_analytics table stays authoritative for operational writes.

  3. 03 Fabric Mirroring

    Continuous replication

    LogMiner redo flows through the on-premises gateway with no ETL schedule to maintain.

  4. 04 OneLake

    One analytics copy

    Delta tables become available to Fabric engines without moving the source workload.

  5. 05 Semantic model

    Governed KPI layer

    Direct Lake exposes 15 shared measures for churn, revenue risk, service quality, and cost.

  6. 06 Fabric Data Agent

    Questions in plain language

    Leaders query governed measures and row-level detail directly or through Microsoft 365 Copilot.

How to pitch it

Lead with a business decision, then explain why Fabric can answer it consistently and safely.

Lead with revenue

Find revenue at risk before it becomes churn.

Show calls that mention a competitor and request cancellation, then join those signals to billing context. A transcript-only tool cannot calculate the recurring revenue attached to those conversations.

Demo question How much annual revenue is at risk from competitor-related cancellation calls?
Prove consistency

Give every team the same definition.

Save rate, escalation rate, first-contact resolution, and revenue at risk live as reviewed measures in the semantic model—not as fresh SQL invented for every question.

Customer value

Operations, finance, and leadership see the same governed answer.

Reduce data friction

Keep Oracle. Remove the copy-pipeline burden.

Mirroring continuously reads Oracle redo into OneLake. There is no watermark logic, incremental-load schedule, or second ingestion estate to operate.

Customer value

Modern analytics without forcing a source-system migration first.
